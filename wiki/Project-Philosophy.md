# Project philosophy

> **Scripture first. Simplicity over features.**

Bible is a free and open-source application for reading and studying Scripture. Our goal is not to build the most complicated Bible study tool, but to make biblical reading **natural, focused and comfortable** — while keeping useful study resources close at hand.

This philosophy guides the interface, development priorities and decisions about new features.

## 1. Simplicity over features

**More features do not automatically make a better application.**

We consider a feature worth adding when it:

1. **Solves a real problem** in reading or studying Scripture.
2. **Fits naturally** into the existing reading experience.
3. **Remains easy to find and use**, including for people who are not technical experts.
4. **Respects the biblical text**, rather than competing with it for attention.
5. **Works reliably**, without making everyday tasks unnecessarily complicated.

If a feature adds clutter, confusing controls or maintenance costs that outweigh its value, we would rather simplify it, rethink it or leave it out.

We also prefer improving an existing workflow over adding a separate screen or setting for every possible use case.

## 2. Scripture first

Reading is the heart of Bible.

Notes, highlights, references, translation comparisons and commentaries are there to **support understanding**, not to dominate the interface. They should be available when needed and stay out of the way when they are not.

This is why we value a clean reading area, adjustable typography, a collapsible study panel and an immersive reading mode.

**The biblical text should remain the main focus, even as Bible gains more powerful study tools.**

## 3. Native, accessible and comfortable

Bible is built for **GNOME**, using **GTK 4 and libadwaita**, rather than imitating another platform's interface.

We aim for:

- Familiar GNOME interactions and visual consistency.
- Clear navigation and discoverable controls.
- Keyboard access for frequent tasks.
- Comfortable reading through adjustable typography, spacing and colors.
- Support for system appearance and high-contrast preferences.
- An interface available in **English and French**.

Accessibility is a continuing responsibility, not something added at the end. We welcome reports about controls that are difficult to see, understand or operate.

See [Appearance and accessibility](Appearance-and-Accessibility).

## 4. Offline reading and user control

After downloading Bible translations and study resources, you should be able to use them **without an internet connection**.

Bible keeps personal study data locally. Our aim is to respect the reader's control over their notes, settings and resources, without requiring an online account just to read Scripture.

We want resources to be easy to discover and install through the application, rather than expecting ordinary users to understand technical module formats.

## 5. Free software and responsible resources

Bible is developed in public under the **GNU General Public License version 2**. Anyone can inspect the source code, report problems and contribute improvements under the project's licensing terms.

The application and the texts it downloads are not the same thing: **Bible translations and commentaries have their own licenses and attribution requirements.** The current library focuses on freely available resources whose distribution terms permit their inclusion.

We believe those distinctions should be clear to users.

The project can be supported voluntarily through [Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/BibleStudyApp). Financial support is not required to use Bible and does not change our commitment to free and open-source software.

## 6. How we evaluate suggestions

Feature proposals are welcome, but **every suggestion is evaluated against the same principles**, whether it comes from a contributor, a regular user or a financial supporter.

When proposing a feature, tell us what you want to accomplish, why the current experience does not solve it well and how a simpler solution might work. We care more about the underlying reading or study need than the number of options a proposal introduces.

We are happy to say **“not now”** to a feature that does not yet fit. Keeping Bible coherent matters more than growing the feature list.
